# Inner Nook Shorts Pack

A Shorts-only plan for **Inner Nook**, a faceless psychology channel in the same niche as the reference channel. It has a playbook, 30 video ideas, and 12 ready-to-record scripts.

---

## 1. Shorts playbook

**Format**
- 9:16 vertical, **25–45 seconds** (about 70–120 spoken words at a calm ~150 words per minute).
- One idea per Short. If you need a list longer than three items, split it into two Shorts.
- Burn in captions. Most viewers scroll with the sound off.
- Keep the same look in every video: one mascot (a soft blob character or a small lamp), a pastel background, and the same font. That consistency is how viewers start to recognise the channel.

**The first 2 seconds decide everything.** Put the hook on screen *and* in the voiceover at the same time. Hook formulas that work in this niche:

| Formula | Example |
|---|---|
| "If you do X, it might mean Y" | "If you apologize for everything, this is for you." |
| Name the effect | "This is called the Spotlight Effect, and it's why you replay that awkward moment." |
| Callout | "POV: you're the friend everyone vents to." |
| Myth-bust | "Being 'low-maintenance' isn't the compliment you think it is." |
| Quick test | "Answer fast: do you remember names, or faces?" |
| Secret or trick | "A psychology trick to make someone like you instantly." |

**Structure of every script**
1. **Hook** (0–2s): the claim or question.
2. **Explain** (2–25s): the named concept, an everyday example, and one line about the research.
3. **Reframe or tip** (25–35s): the "so what", i.e. what the viewer can do with it.
4. **Loop or CTA** (last 2–3s): end on a line that leads back into the first line, or on "Follow for part 2".

**Rules for staying trustworthy (and staying monetisable)**
- Only name a study or researcher if you can link it in the description. Every script below uses real, well-known concepts.
- Say "may", "can" and "is linked to". Never diagnose the viewer.
- For mental-health topics, add a pinned comment: "Not medical advice. If you're struggling, please reach out to a professional."

**Posting rhythm:** 1 Short a day if you can keep it up, or at least 4 a week. Run them as series ("Psychology Effects #1, #2…", "Signs You Were the Easy Child, Part 1/2/3"). Series get viewers to come back and binge.

**Cheap ways to make these**
- **Almost free:** still images plus a text-to-speech voiceover plus captions in CapCut or Canva. Add slow zooms and pans to each image. Many successful faceless psychology Shorts are made exactly like this.
- **Low-cost AI video:** a budget video model at 720p costs about **10 credits per 10 seconds**, so about 30–40 credits for a 30–40 second Short. That's roughly 25 Shorts a month on the Plus plan's 1,000 credits.

## 3. Ready-to-record scripts

Each script has **HOOK TEXT** (the big on-screen text), **VO** (the voiceover), **VISUALS** (one line per cut), and a **CAPTION** for the upload. They run about 30–40 seconds each.

---

### Script 1: The Spotlight Effect
**HOOK TEXT:** Nobody noticed. Seriously.
**VO:**
You tripped in public and replayed it all night. Here's the thing: almost nobody noticed.
Psychologists call this the **Spotlight Effect**, our tendency to overestimate how much other people notice us.
In one well-known study, students wore an embarrassing T-shirt into a room. They guessed about half the room noticed. The real number was far lower.
Everyone else is busy starring in their own movie.
So next time you cringe at yourself, remember: you're the only one still watching the replay.
**VISUALS:** blob trips → huge spotlight on it → the spotlight shrinks to a tiny dot → the other blobs are all looking at their own phones or mirrors → the blob relaxes.
**CAPTION:** You're the only one replaying it 🎬 #psychology #overthinking #spotlighteffect
*Source: Gilovich, Medvec & Savitsky (2000), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.*

---

### Script 2: The Ben Franklin Effect
**HOOK TEXT:** Want someone to like you? Ask THEM for a favor.
**VO:**
Want someone to like you more? Don't do them a favor. Ask them for one.
It's called the **Ben Franklin Effect**. Franklin wrote about winning over a rival by asking to borrow a rare book.
The idea: when we help someone, our brain explains it with "I must like them."
Keep it small, like borrowing a pen or asking for a recommendation.
Small asks, big connection. Try it this week.
**VISUALS:** blob hands another blob a gift, which gets shrugged off → cut to the blob asking to borrow a book → the other blob's heart meter rises → both blobs laughing.
**CAPTION:** The favor trick 📚 #psychologytricks #benfranklineffect #socialskills
*Source: Franklin's autobiography; Jecker & Landy (1969), Human Relations.*

---

### Script 3: The Pratfall Effect
**HOOK TEXT:** Your mistakes might make you MORE likable
**VO:**
Being perfect might be making you less likable.
In a classic experiment, people listened to a highly competent quiz contestant. When he spilled coffee on himself, listeners actually liked him more.
It's called the **Pratfall Effect**. A small, human mistake makes a capable person feel relatable.
The key word is *capable*. It works when people already see you as competent.
So stop hiding every flaw. It might be your most charming feature.
**VISUALS:** a perfect shiny blob on a pedestal, with the others looking away → it spills coffee → the others laugh warmly and walk closer.
**CAPTION:** Perfect is overrated ☕ #psychology #confidence #pratfalleffect
*Source: Aronson, Willerman & Floyd (1966), Psychonomic Science.*

---

### Script 4: The Zeigarnik Effect
**HOOK TEXT:** Why unfinished tasks won't leave your head
**VO:**
Ever notice unfinished tasks just won't leave your head?
That's the **Zeigarnik Effect**. Psychologist Bluma Zeigarnik noticed that waiters remembered unpaid orders perfectly, then forgot them once the bill was paid.
Our brains keep open loops running in the background.
Here's the hack: when you can't finish something, write down the very next step. Research suggests making a plan helps your mind let go.
Close the loop… or at least write it down.
**VISUALS:** blob in bed, thought bubbles full of to-dos → a waiter blob juggling order tickets → the blob writes "next step" on a sticky note → the thought bubbles pop and it falls asleep.
**CAPTION:** Your brain hates open loops 🔁 #productivity #psychology #overthinking
*Sources: Zeigarnik (1927); Masicampo & Baumeister (2011), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology.*

---

### Script 5: Negativity Bias
**HOOK TEXT:** 10 compliments. 1 insult. Guess which you remember.
**VO:**
Ten compliments, one rude comment. Which one are you still thinking about?
That's **negativity bias**. Our brains give bad information more weight than good.
It likely helped our ancestors survive. Missing a threat cost more than missing a treat.
But today it means one comment can outweigh a whole day of kindness.
Try this: when something good happens, pause and actually savor it for a few seconds. Help it stick.
And hey, this comment section? Let's make it a nice one.
**VISUALS:** ten small hearts bounce off the blob → one spiky comment sticks to it → a caveman blob running from a tiger → the blob holding a warm glowing moment → a comment-section graphic.
**CAPTION:** Why the bad sticks 🧠 #negativitybias #mentalhealth #psychology
*Source: Baumeister et al. (2001), "Bad Is Stronger Than Good", Review of General Psychology.*

### Script 6: 3 Signs You Were the "Easy Child"
**HOOK TEXT:** Were you the "easy" kid? 3 signs.
**VO:**
Were you the "easy" kid growing up? Here are three signs it still shows.
One: asking for help feels like a burden. You'll struggle alone before you "bother" anyone.
Two: you read a room instantly. A sigh, a slammed door, and you already know the mood.
Three: your default answer is "I'm fine," even when you're not.
Being easy was a skill you learned. But you're allowed to have needs now.
Which one got you?
**VISUALS:** blob with a gold "Low-Maintenance" sticker → deleting a "can you help me" text → weather icons over other blobs → hiding a huge cast behind its back → the sticker peels off.
**CAPTION:** You were never too much 🫶 #innerchild #psychology #childhood
**Part 2 idea:** you take care of everyone's feelings, you don't know what you want, and praise feels awkward.

### Script 7: 3 Signs You're the "Therapist Friend"
**HOOK TEXT:** POV: you're the friend everyone vents to
**VO:**
If you're the friend everyone comes to with their problems… this is for you.
Sign one: people open up to you within minutes of meeting you.
Sign two: you know everyone's struggles, but nobody really knows yours.
Sign three: you feel guilty when you're the one who needs support.
Being a great listener is a gift. But friendship should go both ways.
Tag the friend who always listens to you. Maybe today, you listen back.
**VISUALS:** blob on a therapist-style couch with a friend blob lying down → a line of blobs waiting → the blob alone with a thought bubble no one sees → a friend hands it a cup of tea.
**CAPTION:** Who checks on the listener? 💬 #therapistfriend #friendship #mentalhealth

### Script 8: Emotionally Exhausted vs. Tired
**HOOK TEXT:** Slept 9 hours. Still exhausted?
**VO:**
You slept nine hours and still feel drained? You might be emotionally exhausted, not just tired.
Signs include: small tasks feel huge, you get irritated faster than usual, and even fun plans feel like a chore.
Sleep restores your body. Emotional exhaustion often needs something else: rest from people-pleasing, pressure, or holding everything together.
Try one "nothing" block this week: no tasks, no replies, no guilt.
And if it lasts weeks, talking to a professional can really help.
**VISUALS:** blob waking up with a full "sleep" bar but an empty "emotional" battery → a tiny task drawn as a mountain → it cancels plans → it lies in a cozy nook with its phone face down.
**CAPTION:** Rest isn't only sleep 🔋 #burnout #selfcare #mentalhealth

### Script 9: The Frequency Illusion
**HOOK TEXT:** You learn a new word… now it's EVERYWHERE
**VO:**
You learn a new word, and suddenly you see it everywhere. Coincidence?
Nope. It's the **frequency illusion**, also called the Baader-Meinhof phenomenon.
Your brain just flagged something as important, so your attention now catches it more often. Then confirmation bias kicks in: "See? It's everywhere!"
Fun fact: now that you know about it… you're going to start noticing it everywhere.
**VISUALS:** blob reading a new word → the word pops up on a billboard, a cereal box, a TV → a magnifying glass over the blob's brain → wink at the camera.
**CAPTION:** You'll see this everywhere now 👀 #psychologyfacts #brain #baadermeinhof
*Term coined by linguist Arnold Zwicky (2005).*

### Script 10: Name It to Tame It
**HOOK TEXT:** A 5-second trick to calm big emotions
**VO:**
Here's a five-second trick for big emotions: name them.
Not just "I feel bad," but "I feel embarrassed," or "I feel left out."
Brain-imaging research found that putting feelings into words, called **affect labeling**, reduced activity in the amygdala, the brain's alarm system.
The more specific the word, the better it tends to work.
So next time your chest gets tight, pause and ask: what exactly am I feeling right now?
**VISUALS:** a storm cloud over the blob → the blob holds up a label reading "embarrassed" → the cloud shrinks → a brain graphic with a small alarm quieting down → the sun comes out.
**CAPTION:** Name it to tame it 🏷️ #emotionalintelligence #anxiety #mentalhealth
*Source: Lieberman et al. (2007), Psychological Science.*

### Script 11: The Peak-End Rule
**HOOK TEXT:** Your brain doesn't remember the whole day
**VO:**
Your brain doesn't remember experiences the way you think.
According to the **peak-end rule**, we mostly judge an experience by its most intense moment and how it ended, not by the average.
Studies by Daniel Kahneman and colleagues found that people remembered a slightly longer unpleasant experience as *better* when it ended more gently.
So if you want a date, a trip, or even a hard conversation to be remembered well…
Make the ending good.
**VISUALS:** a timeline of a blob's day as a wavy line → a spike at the best moment glows → the ending glows → the blob waves goodbye warmly at a door.
**CAPTION:** End on a high note 🎢 #psychology #kahneman #memory
*Source: Kahneman et al. (1993), Psychological Science.*

### Script 12: Quick Test: Names or Faces?
**HOOK TEXT:** Answer FAST: names or faces?
**VO:**
Quick, don't think: do you remember names or faces better?
Most people say faces, and there's a reason. Recognizing a face is easier than recalling a name. Recognition just asks "have I seen this before?" Recall makes you pull the answer from memory with no cue.
Tip: when you learn a name, say it back right away and link it to something: "Maya, like my cousin."
Now tell me in the comments: names or faces?
**VISUALS:** big "NAMES vs FACES" split screen → a face lighting up with ✓ → an empty name tag with "?" → the blob shaking hands and saying the name aloud, and a name tag appears → a comment prompt.
**CAPTION:** Comment NAMES or FACES 👇 #memory #psychologyfacts #quiz

## 4. Title and packaging tips

- Keep on-screen hook text to **8 words or fewer**, big, and in the top third of the frame so the Shorts interface doesn't cover it.
- The first frame *is* your thumbnail, so start on the hook-text frame and not on a blank one.
- Title template: `[Effect or claim] + emoji` (e.g. "Nobody noticed your embarrassing moment 😳").
- Put 3–5 hashtags in the description. Always include #psychology and #shorts, plus one topic tag.
- Pin a comment that asks a question (e.g. "Which one are you?") to get replies.
